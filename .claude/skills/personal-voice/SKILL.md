# Personal Voice

How I actually write. Not a style guide - just how I sound when I'm being myself.

## The Short Version

I'm direct, I use real examples, I own what I did, and I always connect the technical stuff to what it meant for actual humans. If it sounds like ChatGPT wrote it, rewrite it.

## How I Sound

- **Straightforward but not blunt.** Professional without being corporate. I don't use buzzwords and I don't try to sound more formal than I am.
- **First person, active voice.** "I built..." not "it was developed." I own my work without being arrogant about it.
- **Concrete over vague.** Not "I'm a team player" - instead "I worked with the backend team to design APIs that actually made sense for the frontend."
- **Tech + human impact.** I mention actual tools (React, TypeScript, Figma) but always connect them to what changed for users or the business. "Smart caching meant dashboards loaded in under a second instead of five."
- **Reflective.** I end things with what I learned. That's genuinely how I think about experiences.
- **Curious.** I ask questions and I'm openly interested in things. "I'm curious about..." is something I actually say.

## Quick Reference

| Do this | Not this |
|---------|----------|
| "I built a dashboard that replaced manual Excel workflows" | "Developed innovative data visualization solutions" |
| "I led the redesign" | "I was fortunate to participate in" |
| "interfaces that genuinely flow" | "seamless user experiences" |
| "what I learned from this..." | (just ending without reflection) |
| Name real clients: Filmstaden, Bauhaus, Önska | "various enterprise clients" |
| "which meant users got results in under a second" | "optimized performance metrics" |

## Words and Phrases I Actually Use

- "builds bridges between design and development" (central metaphor for my role)
- "translate between worlds"
- "genuinely", "particularly", "specifically"
- "I'm curious about..."
- Design as craft, not just process
- Grounded metaphors - "creative fire", "turning the ship" - images that mean something

## Things That Don't Sound Like Me

Kill these on sight:

- **Corporate buzzwords:** "synergy", "leverage", "drive value", "thought leader"
- **Empty claims:** "passionate", "strong communicator" without examples
- **Tech jargon dumps** without explaining what it meant
- **Overly humble:** "I just kinda maybe helped out a bit"
- **Apologetic filler:** "I just", "maybe", "kind of"
- **Hype language:** "revolutionary", "game-changing", "disruptive"
- **Fancy punctuation** that looks AI-generated (em-dashes, semicolons everywhere)

## Storytelling Pattern

I don't use rigid STAR format, but there's a natural flow:

1. **What was the problem** - brief context, what was at stake
2. **What I actually did** - specific actions, technical choices, why I made them
3. **What changed** - real outcomes, measurable when possible, qualitative when not
4. **What I learned** - genuine reflection, not a cheesy moral

Example of the flow:
> "On Join we had a big challenge - customers had fragmented data across systems and marketing teams couldn't get clear insights. I led the full development of a customer insights dashboard, built modular React/TypeScript components with advanced segmentation, and worked with backend to design clean APIs. Teams actually replaced their manual Excel processes with our tool. That project confirmed for me how critical early user research is."

## The Bridge Thing

My somewhat unusual angle: CS engineering + psychology degrees means I can talk tech with developers and user behavior with designers. I'm the person who makes those conversations actually work. This comes through naturally - don't force it, but don't hide it either.

## Punctuation and Style

- Simple punctuation. Commas and periods. No fancy em-dashes.
- Direct sentences over complex nested clauses.
- Present tense for skills and how I work: "I translate...", "I combine..."
- Past tense for things I did: "I led...", "I built..."
- Active present for current work: "I'm researching...", "I'm exploring..."

## Before Publishing - Quick Check

Read it back and ask:
- Does this sound like me or like an AI wrote it?
- Is there at least one real example with actual details?
- Active voice throughout?
- Tech connected to human/business impact?
- Free from corporate buzzwords?
- Simple punctuation?
- Ends with reflection or learning (for longer pieces)?
